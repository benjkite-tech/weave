02What they are & why most failThe difference between a value and a platitude+

What they are

Tie-breakers for when two good options conflict and there's no data left to settle it. Not a vibe — a decision rule. "When speed and polish collide, we ship and learn" is a value. "Excellence" is a mood.

Why they matter

They let people make the call you'd make without asking. Done right, they scale judgement and speed up decisions. Done wrong, they're a trust tax — everyone sees the gap between the poster and the behaviour.

Why most fail

Invented at an offsite as agreeable nouns that cost nothing and live nowhere but a wall. Nobody ever got fired for violating "Integrity." A value that can't be broken isn't a value — it's a platitude.

VALUES AS LEVERAGE, NOT LAMINATE

The companies whose values actually work all do the same thing: each one rules something specific out. That's the cost — and the clarity.

Netflix
"Dream Team"
A pro sports team, not a family. The "keeper test" rules out keeping a loyal but fading performer out of sentiment.
DoorDash
"Lowest level of detail"
Leaders dig into the specifics — even the CEO does deliveries. Rules out managing from the slide deck.
Amazon
"Day 1"
Rules out treating past success as permission to slow down or stop inventing.
Patagonia
"Cause no unnecessary harm"
Rules out profitable product lines that aren't environmentally sound.

GATHER — START WITH BEHAVIOURS

Don't ask "what do we believe?" Ask "what do people actually do here — or what do we want them to?" Works whether you're refreshing values or founding from scratch. Answer what you can. AI-assisted

Make. What we build & who for → Want. The behaviour we want to be known for → Proven. A choice that shows it's real → Refuse. What we won't tolerate
What we build forces values: a premium maker can't value "good enough"; a safety-critical one can't value "move fast." New company? Describe what you're setting out to build.
At our best, what do people actually do? Describe the action, not a noun. New company? Describe the behaviour you want to be true from day one.
A decision that proves the value cost you something. New company? Name a line you've already decided you'll hold, even when it's expensive.
What gets someone managed out, even if they're brilliant? A value is defined as much by what it rules out.

SHAPE — MAKE THEM SHARP

Each value is scored against three tests. Low scores mean a dead value pretending to be alive — rewrite until it can settle a real argument. AI-assisted

01

Is it costly?

A real value makes you say no to something attractive. If it never forces a sacrifice, it's decoration.

02

Is it specific?

Could it settle a real argument at 5pm on a Friday? "Disagree and commit" can; "Teamwork" can't.

03

Defensible opposite?

"Team, not family" works because firms sincerely choose family. If no one would pick the opposite, you've said nothing.

PRIORITISE — CUT, THEN RANK THE COLLISIONS

The hardest part of values work, and the part most tools skip. First cut to a real set (4–6 — fewer than you think). Then settle the tensions: when two of your values pull in opposite directions, which one wins?

1 Make the cut

Keep the ones that are genuinely true and load-bearing. A tight set of 4–6 beats a wall of 10 nobody remembers. Untick to drop.

2 Settle the tensions

For each pair that can genuinely conflict, pick which wins when you can't honour both. This is the hierarchy a team can actually use at 5pm on a Friday — the single most useful thing to come out of a values exercise.
